Scoring a prediction of a number

Choose the metric before you see the answer, and always report it beside a baseline.

import numpy as np

On this card: y is what happened and p the prediction, two arrays of the same length.

np.abs(y - p).mean()
MAE, mean absolute error: the average size of a mistake, in the units of the target.Session 4
((y - p) ** 2).mean()
MSE, mean squared error: one large miss dominates it, which is sometimes exactly what you want.Session 4
np.sqrt(((y - p) ** 2).mean())
RMSE, the root of the MSE, back in the units of the target. The one to report.Session 4
1 - ((y - p) ** 2).sum() / ((y - y.mean()) ** 2).sum()
R squared: how much better than always predicting the average. Zero means no better at all, and out of sample it can be negative.Session 4
np.full(len(y), y_train.mean())
The baseline prediction: one number, the training average, repeated. Nothing counts as a result until it beats this.Session 4

Scoring a prediction of a label

No residuals here. Four counts, and every classification metric is built out of them.

On this card: said and was are columns of True and False, the prediction and what happened; tp, fp, fn and tn are the four counts.

(said & was).sum()
True positives. False positives are (said & ~was), misses (~said & was), true negatives (~said & ~was).Session 4
(tp + tn) / (tp + fp + fn + tn)
Accuracy: the share called correctly. Meaningless when one class is rare.Session 4
tp / (tp + fp)
Precision: of the times the model said yes, how often was it right? Low precision means false alarms.Session 4
tp / (tp + fn)
Recall: of the times it really happened, how many were caught? Low recall means misses.Session 4
2 * precision * recall / (precision + recall)
F1: the harmonic mean of the two, which sits close to the smaller one.Session 4
