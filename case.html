One investigation, Parts 1 to 12

The case

You are a junior analyst building a risk report on eleven US instruments. The report grows after most sessions, a part at a time, from two stocks compared by hand in Part 1 to every model of the course side by side in Part 12.

How it works

  • A part after most sessions. Parts are numbered by session, so there is no Part 7, 10 or 13: those sessions are case lectures of their own.
  • Each part starts where the last one stopped. From Part 2 on, a quick load cell restores the numbers the earlier parts worked out, so you can start a part even if you skipped the one before.
  • Do the session's exercises first. The exercises teach the moves; the case asks you to choose which move to make.
  • Run all is safe. Every cell runs before you fill in its ..., and hints and solutions are folded under each question.

Where it ends

Parts 1 to 3 described a year that had happened. Part 4 turned the report into a question about the next month and set the rules every later part kept: a target that cannot see the future, a split by date, and baselines to beat. Parts 5 to 12 then brought one family of models after another to that question, and each was held to the same test block and the same folds.

The report that goes out holds a volatility forecast, a warning before a jump, and the evidence for both. On the folds, the one-column model from Part 5 was never beaten by enough to replace it.
